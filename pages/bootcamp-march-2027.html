How your child will learn

Build with AI. Learn to make the decisions.

This course helps your child turn an idea into a working app they can understand and explain. Along the way, they learn to direct AI, test its work and make their own decisions.

Guidance from university and industry mentors

University educators and industry mentors deliver the course in person. They demonstrate new concepts, answer questions and give feedback as students develop their projects step by step.

Decisions your child makes

Your child chooses what the app should do, gives AI instructions and reviews its suggestions. They learn how a feature works and explain why they accepted or changed an AI-generated result.

Results they learn to check

Students test whether their app works as intended. For a quiz, that might mean trying correct and incorrect answers and checking the score. They investigate problems, verify improvements and explain what they found.

What your child could build

Your child will choose a project from three categories, with instructor guidance to keep it achievable within the week. Each category offers a small selection of project ideas, with room for their own interests and decisions.

Games

A game with clear rules, player actions and a score or result.

Project options: A memory-matching game or a reaction-time challenge.

Your child chooses the theme and shapes the rules, difficulty and player experience.

Learning Tools

An app that helps someone practise a topic or check their understanding.

Project options: A practice quiz or a flashcard app.

Your child chooses the subject, develops and checks the content, and decides how questions and feedback appear.

Everyday Tools

A useful app that helps someone organise a task or manage their time.

Project options: A study timer or a task-priority helper.

Your child decides who the tool is for, how it should work and what makes it useful.

Each student develops one main project, starting with two or three essential features, then testing and improving it with instructor guidance.

Your child’s week, day by day

Each day combines instructor demonstrations, hands-on building, feedback and time to explain what students are learning.

March 15–19, 2027 · From idea to launch
Monday
March 151

Discover and create

Students explore how AI can help build an app and where its results need checking. They create a first mini-app, request a change and test what happens.

Tuesday
March 162

Choose and plan

Your child selects a project, sketches the experience and chooses two or three essential features. With instructor guidance, they review AI’s proposed plan before building.

Wednesday
March 173

Build and understand

Students develop their app one feature at a time, checking and saving progress. They learn to explain how a user’s action produces a result on screen.

Thursday
March 184

Test and improve

Students exchange feedback with a peer and investigate problems in their app. They use AI to help make repairs, then test whether the changes work.

Friday
March 195

Launch and share

With instructor support, students prepare their app for sharing and publish where approved. They demonstrate their project, explain important decisions and describe how AI helped.

QUESTIONS PARENTS ASK

Everything you need to know.

Does my child need coding experience?

No. The program is designed for students ages 13–16 with no prior coding experience. Instructors help students get started, communicate their ideas to AI and understand the essential behaviour of their app.

What will my child build?

Students begin with a mini-app, then develop an individual browser app with instructor guidance. Possible projects include a game, learning tool or interactive story. Instructors help keep the project achievable within the week.

Will my child keep their project code and app?

Yes. Your child will receive a GitHub repository containing their project code and version history, plus a live link to their deployed app that they can open and share.

How do you check that students understand what they build?

Students explain their goals, review AI suggestions and test the results. During short Show · Explain · Change checkpoints, they demonstrate a feature, explain their thinking and respond to a small new requirement. Instructors use these conversations to guide feedback and support.

Does my child need to bring a laptop?

No. Future Wrights provides a MacBook for use during the program, along with the required course software, Codex and OpenAI API access.

Is this a full-day program?

Students attend one three-hour session each day, Monday–Friday, March 15–19, 2027. Choose either the morning group, 9:00 AM–12:00 PM, or the afternoon group, 1:00–4:00 PM.

Will my child’s app be available online?

Students work toward publishing a small web app with instructor guidance. Online sharing follows agreed consent and privacy arrangements. Where publication is approved, students receive a live link others can use to try their app.

How do you handle privacy and consent?

Student projects may be shared through live web links and GitHub repositories. Before publication, we explain what will be publicly visible and request parent or guardian consent.

Students are guided to use fictional sample data in their projects and avoid sharing personal information with AI tools or including it in published work.

Permission to use a student’s name, photograph, video or testimonial in promotional materials is requested separately.

For questions or to discuss sharing preferences, contact admissions@futurewrights.ai.

What does the tuition include?

The $999 CAD tuition, including applicable taxes, covers the five-day bootcamp, use of a MacBook, required course software and access, a certificate of completion and a personalised learning report.

How do we apply?

Select Apply Now to submit an application. Each group has up to 15 students, with applications accepted on a rolling basis until full.

If you would like to discuss the program first, book a 20-minute parent information session or email admissions@futurewrights.ai.

What is your refund and cancellation policy?

All cancellations and refunds are subject to the following policy:

  • Cancellation requests: All cancellations must be submitted in writing by email to admissions@futurewrights.ai.
  • At least 30 days before the cohort starts: A refund will be issued, less a $100 CAD administrative fee.
  • Fewer than 30 days before the cohort starts: No refunds will be provided.
  • Missed days: Pro-rated refunds or credits are not available for days absent.
  • Cancellation by Future Wrights Academy: We reserve the right to cancel a cohort if minimum enrolment is not met or exceptional or unforeseen circumstances arise. If we cancel, we will accommodate the student in an alternate cohort or provide a full refund.
